# What are SME's knowledge gaps regarding AI in the sector of Waterkracht?

!!! warning "Overall confidence: MEDIUM"
    Approved by **Kyran Weijman** on 2026-10-19.
    Research last updated 2026-10-07.


## AI Knowledge Gaps for SMEs in the Waterkracht Sector

## Research question
What are SME's knowledge gaps regarding AI in the sector of Waterkracht?

## Business relevance
Waterkracht B.V. designs, assembles, sells and services industrial equipment. Across Europe and the Netherlands, SMEs in comparable technical and manufacturing sectors are adopting AI more slowly than larger firms. Understanding exactly which knowledge gaps hold back adoption helps Waterkracht decide where to invest first: training, data cleanup, or external advice. No source reviewed names Waterkracht, hydropower, or water-technology equipment specifically. All findings below come from general SME, manufacturing, or Dutch-economy research and must be treated as indicative, not sector-confirmed.

## Key findings

- **Dutch AI adoption is still low for small firms.** Among Dutch microbusinesses (2-9 employees), AI use rose from 6.8% (2023) to 13.8% (2025). Adoption grows with firm size: 11.6% at 2 employees up to 19.0% at 5-9 employees, and 29.8% for firms with 10-249 employees. Construction (4.6%) and hospitality (4.2%) show low adoption; no figure is given for industrial equipment manufacturing or water technology (CBS, 2026).

- **Three recurring adoption barriers in manufacturing SMEs.** A German study of manufacturing SMEs (mechanical engineering, metal, plastics, electronics, automotive parts - not hydropower) found three dominant gaps: lack of in-house AI expertise or resources (72% of respondents), incompatibility of AI with existing systems/software (54%), and poor availability or quality of data (53%) (Knoblach et al., 2025). These are general manufacturing findings, not Dutch or water-sector specific, but the three categories - people, IT systems, data - are a useful checklist for any SME, including Waterkracht.

- **Dutch policy research confirms the same pattern.** A Dutch Ministry of Economic Affairs-commissioned study found "lack of knowledge and skills" (gebrek aan kennis en vaardigheden) is a key barrier to AI adoption in Dutch SMEs, alongside limited digitalization, cost, and fear of job loss. Technical sectors (technische sectoren) and small firms lag due to lack of time, resources, or expertise. SMEs often lack insight into what AI can concretely offer their business, lack structured data, and lack technical, legal, and organizational knowledge. Experts say developing a vision/strategy is the most critical first step (Vankan et al., Dialogic, 2025). The report does not name Waterkracht or the hydropower sector.

- **Specific gap areas named by a national training initiative.** Smart Industry NL, with FME, AIC4NL and AI-Certified, launched a free online AI learning platform for the Dutch manufacturing industry. By building role-based courses on basic AI literacy, the EU AI Act's impact on companies, and human-AI collaboration, the initiative implies these are the gaps Dutch manufacturing SMEs currently have (Smart Industry NL, undated). This is a promotional announcement, not a study, so treat the implied gaps as plausible, not proven.

- **Trust, security and ethics gaps in generative AI.** An academic framework paper states that SMEs generally "lack comprehensive understanding of how to securely and responsibly implement AI technologies within their operational contexts," pointing to gaps in AI/LLM security, ethical use, and risk management (Sarker et al., 2025). This is generic across sectors and not verified with sector-specific data in what was reviewed.

## Risks
- Acting on these findings as if they describe Waterkracht directly would be a mistake: none of the five sources mention Waterkracht, hydropower, or water-technology equipment. Extrapolating general manufacturing or Dutch SME findings to Waterkracht's specific situation is "not stated" and should be checked internally.
- Any reference above to the EU AI Act reflects only a training-platform announcement's framing of the law's relevance, not a legal analysis (unchecked against primary legal text).
- Two sources (Smart Industry NL, Sarker et al.) have a built-in promotional or self-interest angle (selling a training platform or a proposed framework), which may overstate the size of the gap they claim to solve.
- The Dialogic/Ministry of Economic Affairs study's sample may be biased toward SMEs already engaged with AI support programs (its own stated limitation), which could understate the true gap for average technical SMEs.
- The Bamberg/manufacturing-SME percentages (72%, 54%, 53%) come from a German regional study, not a Dutch or water-sector sample; applying these numbers directly to Waterkracht is not supported by the source.

## Checklist
Use this as a starting self-check for Waterkracht, based on the gap categories found above:

- [ ] Do we have at least one person who understands, in practical terms, what AI can and cannot do for our equipment business? (expertise gap)
- [ ] Have we checked whether AI tools would work with our current software and machines, or would require new IT investment? (compatibility gap)
- [ ] Do we have clean, structured, accessible data (e.g. machine logs, maintenance records, sales data) that an AI tool could actually use? (data gap)
- [ ] Do we understand what the EU AI Act might require from a company like ours? (unchecked against primary legal text)
- [ ] Do we have a basic vision or strategy for why we would use AI, beyond "everyone else is doing it"?
- [ ] Have we discussed security, data protection, and responsible use before trying any AI tool, including generative AI/chatbots?

## Next action
Ask the IT or operations lead at Waterkracht to walk through the checklist above in a one-hour internal meeting, and write down, honestly, which boxes are unchecked - this is the cheapest possible way to find out which specific knowledge gap to address first, before spending money on training or tools.
